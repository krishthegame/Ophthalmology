02 Product Impact

Three VoiceStack releases. Three measurable improvements.

In six weeks, three product changes moved the practice's core call metrics. The data below shows the before and after for each.

Release timeline
March–April 2026
10 March
Callback feature implemented
Patients waiting in queue can press 1 to hold their position and receive an automatic callback when their turn arrives — instead of hanging up and never reaching the practice. Before this, the only recovery options were the team calling back (rare) or the patient ringing a second time.
14 April
Callback queue time extended: 10 min → 30 min
A callback request previously timed out after 10 minutes, marking it as "Not Fulfilled." The window was tripled to 30 minutes so the TCO and agents have realistic time to reach the patient. Three days of data show a 22-point lift in callback fulfilment.
16 April
IVR menu removed
The phone tree ("press 1 for appointments…") was removed — calls now go straight to an agent. Patients no longer hang up during a menu they never wanted. Pre-queue drops, previously 6.7–8.1% of all calls, dropped to around 3% the following day — roughly halving the category.

Five patterns the monthly view hides

01

Monday accounts for 40.9% of all operating-hour misses

Monday is the highest-volume day (127 calls/day avg · vs 61 on Friday) and the lowest answer rate (79.2%). That's a structural capacity-demand mismatch, not a one-off. Solving Monday alone would lift the overall answer rate by roughly 5 points with zero other changes.

02

The 3pm floor is universal across the week

Answer rate drops from 94% at 8am to a 79.5% floor at 3pm, then recovers to 95%+ by 5pm. Tracks a predictable afternoon-slump pattern — likely clinical handovers, lunchtime cascades, or admin tasks. Flex staffing to the dip and the floor disappears.

03

New patients at 3pm answer at 49.3%

New-patient AR collapses in the afternoon (60.8% for the 2pm–5pm block vs 78%+ either side). Every missed new-patient call is ~£3–8K lifetime value gone. If this pattern held all year, that's well over 200 new-patient calls lost to the 3pm dip alone.

04

Outbound activity is inverted from need

The team makes the most outbound calls on Thursday (283) — the day with the fewest missed inbound to recover. Monday, which generates 40% of the misses, gets only 233 outbound. Callbacks aren't being worked in proportion to where they're created. Shift outbound focus to Monday/Tuesday mornings and you attack the same missed calls twice.

05

738 Pre-Queue Drops happened during business hours

These are flagged "offline missed" but sit inside 8am–6pm. Monday 9am alone has 54 Pre-Queue Drops — suggesting agents aren't fully logged in at shift start. The first 30 minutes of Monday is leaking calls before the queue even opens. A hard log-in SLA of 8:45am on Mondays would recover most of this.

Other patterns worth flagging

+

Emergency calls answer at 97.8% — the urgency routing works

Of 365 calls tagged Emergency Appointment, only 8 were missed. The urgency sort is a genuine strength — worth crediting explicitly when the broader AR is under pressure. Also a strong VoiceStack proof-point for urgent-first triage.

+

Missed callers are patient — median wait is 51 seconds

Only 37% hang up within 30s. 46% wait longer than a full minute before giving up. These aren't impulsive callers — they're motivated patients being lost to queue depth, not attention span. Even a 20-second improvement in answer time would recover a meaningful share.

+

Voicemail is effectively a dead channel

Of 645 missed calls, only 24 left a voicemail (3.7%). Patients either hang up or self-recover by calling back. Voicemail shouldn't be prioritised in the callback workflow — the callback queue (based on number-level identification) is doing the real work.

+

The worst single days were all early-week

Feb 24 (Tue, 183 calls, 51.9% AR), Mar 2 (Mon, 161, 56.5%), Mar 23 (Mon, 151, 68.2%). Seven of the top-10 busiest days answered below 85%. The answer rate breaks at exactly the moments you can't afford it to — demand-linked, not random noise.

What these patterns imply for the action plan

Highest leverage

Flex-staff Monday 10am–4pm

Add one floating agent across the Monday window. Monday alone creates 40% of misses — fixing this one cell moves the overall AR by ~5 points. Revisit in 4 weeks.

New patient protection

VIP-route new-patient calls

Priority queue tag for new-patient callers (detectable via first-time number). Lifting new-patient AR from 68% to 85% is worth meaningful LTV — disproportionate revenue per agent-hour invested.

Afternoon dip

No lunch/handover gap 2pm–3:30pm

The 3pm floor is a scheduling choice, not a law of physics. Stagger breaks; ensure minimum two agents live across the entire 2pm–3:30pm window every weekday.

Log-in SLA

8:45am hard log-in on Mondays

738 in-hours Pre-Queue Drops tell you agents aren't fully online at shift start. Add a log-in check at 8:45am Monday/Tuesday — recovers 50+ calls/week currently leaking before the queue opens.

Outbound re-balance

Shift callback effort earlier in the week

Callbacks are made most on Thursday but created most on Monday. Move to a same-day / next-morning SLA. You'll hit more callers while the conversation is still fresh — and lift the 31% leakage rate.

Dashboard

Track AR by day × block weekly

The monthly view is too coarse — it hides Monday afternoons. Add a weekly 5×4 heatmap (this page, refreshed) to the team stand-up. Target every amber/red cell ≥ 85% by end of Q2.

07.3 The save rate

How often do agents save a cancellation?

When a patient calls to cancel, a skilled agent can often convert that call into a reschedule instead — offering an alternative time, checking slot availability, understanding the reason. This is pure retained revenue.

Cancellation save rate

Feb–Mar: The team saved roughly one in three cancellation attempts — 34 saves in Feb (29.1%), 57 in Mar (36.8%). That's material retained revenue.

April: Save rate collapsed to 5.7% — only 3 of 53 cancel-intent calls were saved. That's a 31-point drop.

April is a partial month (17 days) and 53 cancel-intent calls is a smaller sample than earlier months — but the directional drop is too steep to be noise. Likely candidates: a change in agent rotation on appointment-update calls, a policy tweak (e.g. less flexibility on re-slots), or a dominant reason shift.
